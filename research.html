Research and publications

I work at the meeting point of information systems, human–AI interaction and AI governance, asking how enterprise AI can adapt to cultural difference without losing its safeguards.

My dissertation

Chair of Societal Computing, TUM, with SAP University Competence Center. Supervised by Prof. Dr. Orestis Papakyriakopoulos. Started January 2026.

Human–AI Interaction Under Cultural Heterogeneity: A Framework for Enterprise AI Systems

Enterprise AI no longer just retrieves information. It recommends, decides and carries out tasks that used to belong to employees. Yet these systems are configured around one set of assumptions about how much authority the AI gets, how much it explains itself and how easily people can challenge it. Those assumptions don't travel evenly across a culturally diverse workforce.

How does cultural heterogeneity shape human–AI interaction, and how can that interaction be designed to support accessible, context-sensitive use across different cultural settings?

The goal is not a catalogue of country-specific AI settings. It is a reusable process that lets organisations adapt enterprise AI to the cultural realities of each location while keeping individual agency, accountability and responsible-AI safeguards intact.

Two ideas at the centre

Enactment bias

A systematic mismatch between the interaction assumptions built into a system and how users in different cultural contexts understand, use and respond to it. It explains why identical deployments can produce unequal patterns of compliance, challenge and control.

CHAI-E

Computer–Human–AI Interaction in Enterprise: a Design Science artifact that turns cultural deployment context into configuration requirements for enterprise-internal AI agents, without mapping cultures to fixed behaviours.

A three-layer
Agentic AI framework

The layers are ordered but iterative: evidence from real use can revise all three.

Layer 1
Cultural interaction How culturally shaped preferences affect decision authority, explanation, and the ability to override or take back control. CHAI-E sits here.
Layer 2
Organizational contingency What the organisation can actually support: roles, processes and accountability structures.
Layer 3
Institutional and governance boundary The legal, ethical and regulatory limits, such as the EU AI Act, that no local adaptation may cross.

Research questions

Each one maps to a research gap and a layer of the framework.

  1. RQ1

    How can culturally informed preferences for human–AI interaction be identified and represented so they can inform the design and configuration of enterprise AI?

    Literature review across culture in information systems, human-centred AI and delegation research; conceptual artifact development using Design Science Research.

  2. RQ2

    How do employees' national-cultural backgrounds shape their interaction preferences toward enterprise AI agents in practice?

    Multiple-case study in a multinational SAP compliance department: a cultural-assessment survey, configuration mock-ups in SAP Joule, focus groups at each location and cross-location comparison.

  3. RQ3

    How must organisational and regulatory boundaries be built in as constraints on culturally adapted interaction, and how far does this generalise beyond one company?

    Review of regional AI regulation such as the EU AI Act, with expert interviews and desk research on other enterprise AI deployments.
